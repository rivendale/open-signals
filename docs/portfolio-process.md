# Portfolio process

## When portfolios change

- **Once a quarter, at filing season**, after earnings and quarterly or annual reports.
- **Any time a written sell rule or kill criterion fires.**
- At no other time. A price move alone is not a reason to review.

## How portfolios are organized

- **Each sleeve has one job**: broad market exposure; a defensive sleeve that holds all the hedges; profitable growth
  companies; a speculative sleeve where each name is small and sized to be lost; and a sleeve for market infrastructure
  (exchanges, brokers, custody, settlement).
- **One home per name**: a stock or theme lives in one sleeve only, so its total exposure is visible.
- **The hedge level of a portfolio is its defensive weight**, instead of hedges spread across sleeves.

## Limits

- **Size:** no single stock above 8% of a sleeve, nothing below 2%. Names in the speculative sleeve stay at 5% or less.
- **Drift:** rebalance a weight only when it moves more than 25% away from its target (a 4% target: below 3% or above
  5%), checked at the quarterly review.
- **Cash:** no standing reserve; additions are funded at the review.

## Taxable and retirement accounts are run differently

| | Retirement accounts | Taxable accounts |
|---|---|---|
| Rebalancing | Active: drift bands and size limits apply at each review | None |
| What changes holdings | The quarterly review and the sell rules | Tax-loss harvesting, small position updates, and new cash |
| New cash | Follows the targets | Buys toward the sleeve's underweights |
| Selling | Sell rules and rebalancing | Only on a fundamental change (a sell rule firing), never drift |
| Holding period | Not a factor | A trim of a position held under a year waits until it is long-term, unless a sell rule fires or it is a loss harvest |

A taxable account reflects when it was opened and what it started with; the target is a guide for new money, not
something to force it back to.

## Placement

- Overlapping funds are consolidated inside tax-advantaged accounts, never by selling at a gain in taxable accounts.
- Real-estate investment trusts belong in tax-advantaged accounts.
- Losses are harvested in taxable accounts first, with the 30-day wash-sale rule observed across every account.
